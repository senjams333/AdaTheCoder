Up and coding in three steps

A grown-up sets things up once, which takes about five minutes. After that, your child can open the app and carry on by themselves, at their own pace, whenever they like.

Hoo! Setting up is easy. I'll be right here if your coder gets stuck.

  1. Make a parent account

    It only takes your name, your email and a password. Your child doesn't need an account or an email address. You can add their first name too, so we can greet them.

  2. Install the app

    Download Ada The Coder for Windows or Mac from your account page and install it like any other program. After that, it works even without the internet.

  3. Let them create

    Each lesson starts with a short video, then examples to click, run and change. When the code works there's a big celebration, and when something goes wrong, a friendly message shows how to fix it.

Questions from parents

My child has never coded. Is that OK?

Yes. The first lesson starts with one line of code, and every lesson explains new words before using them.

Does my child need to be good at reading?

Lessons use short sentences and big text. Younger children who are still learning to read will enjoy it most with a grown-up reading alongside them.

How long does a lesson take?

Each lesson is short: a video and a handful of small examples. Most children can finish one in a single sitting, and they can always stop and pick up where they left off.

Which computers does it work on?

Windows 10 or 11, and Mac computers running macOS 11 or newer. iPads, iPhones and Chromebooks aren't supported yet.

What information do you keep about my child?

Only what you choose to tell us when signing up: an optional first name and age. Your child's code and progress stay on your computer. Read the privacy page for details.

How do updates work?

New lessons and videos download into the app by themselves. When a new version of the app is ready, the app shows a message and a grown-up can download it from this website.
